The public platform

The District has spent generations protecting land. Indy’s campaign asks how that protected land can more fully sustain the people and communities around it.

01 · PROTECT

Care for the land before the emergency.

Keep land, water, roads, trails and fire-prone landscapes healthy and safe — with maintenance and ecological stewardship matched to the places the District manages.

  • Wildfire resilience
  • Wetlands and shorelines
  • Healthy soil and habitat
02 · CONNECT

Make parks work for more people.

Treat parks as public-health and belonging infrastructure, with better access, locally relevant programming and meaningful ways for residents to participate.

  • Health and wellbeing
  • Transit and first-visit access
  • Youth and paid stewardship
03 · DELIVER

Make public money produce visible results.

Modernize field systems, publish priorities and costs, and make it easier to see what is being fixed, restored and improved.

  • Transparent priorities
  • Modern field tools
  • Measurable outcomes
Doughnut Vision 2036

A safe and thriving place for people and planet.

Doughnut Economics asks how we meet more human needs without damaging the living systems we depend on. Indy’s 2036 vision applies that question to Ward 7’s parks and communities.

People thrive

HealthParks that support movement, stress reduction and connection.
FoodExplore edible landscapes and community stewardship where appropriate.
YouthPaid pathways into restoration, stewardship and civic work.
AccessMake it easier for more communities to reach and use parks.

Nature thrives

Fire resilienceProactive vegetation and ecological management.
ShorelinesWetlands and living systems that improve habitat and absorb flooding.
BiodiversityNative plant, pollinator and wildlife corridors.
Circular systemsTurn organic material back into useful ecological value.
The theory of change: If we invest in parks as infrastructure for both people and planet, healthier land can support healthier communities, healthier communities can become stronger stewards, and accountable public systems can reinforce both.

Meet Indy

Parks can do more.

Indy is a regenerative farmer, community organizer and systems thinker running for the East Bay Regional Park District Board in Ward 7.

His work focuses on the relationship between personal health, community wellbeing and the health of the land. He is running because he believes the Park District can build on its legacy of land protection and become a more active partner in public health, climate resilience, ecological restoration and community stewardship.

His question is straightforward: How can the land we have already protected do more good for the people and places around it?

Plain-language FAQ

What does this mean?

Why call it “Doughnut Vision”?

The doughnut is a simple model: make sure people can thrive while keeping ecological systems healthy. The campaign uses it as a long-horizon method, not as a prerequisite for understanding the platform.

Is every 2036 idea already an official Park District program?

No. The site distinguishes campaign proposals and future concepts from existing District programs. Partnerships are not implied until they are actually established.

What does one Park District director control?

One director does not control the District alone. Indy would need to build public support and Board support for pilots, policies and investments.

Why focus on public systems?

Maintenance, ecological care, field data and capital decisions are connected. Better information and clearer priorities can help staff, the Board and residents understand what needs attention and what progress looks like.
